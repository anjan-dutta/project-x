What manufacturers under ₹100 crore actually struggle with

Published evidence on supply chain risk in Indian micro and small manufacturing, gathered to test whether the system design is aimed at a real problem. Every figure is attributed.

How to read this

These figures come from search-engine summaries of the cited reports and their news coverage, not from reading the primary documents. The network used to compile this page could not retrieve the source PDFs directly.

Numbers were cross-checked against multiple outlets where possible, and each one names its study and sample size below. Even so, verify the headline figures against the primary sources before using them in a pitch or an investment memo. Where sources disagree, that disagreement is stated rather than averaged away.

The segment is a legal category

Notification S.O. 1364(E) of 21 March 2025, effective 1 April 2025, revised the MSME thresholds. "Under ₹100 crore turnover" is not an arbitrary cut: it is exactly the ceiling of the Small category, so the target segment is micro plus small enterprises as the state defines them.

Why it matters

The segment maps onto Udyam registration data, statutory 45-day payment protection under the MSMED Act, and government scheme eligibility. All three are usable for targeting, and Udyam is a route into the cold-start problem.

The findings

Price volatility is the number one procurement problem

India SME Forum, Digital Bharat: India Digital Procurement Report 2026 · 27,000+ registered MSMEs · built on Udyam registration data

The same report puts the MSME procurement economy at ₹124.9 trillion, notes that only 30–40% of procurement spend moves through digital channels, and finds that over three-quarters of MSMEs spend ₹10 lakh to ₹1 crore every month on procurement.

Implication

This is third-party validation for the commodity-signal layer, and it sizes the monthly decision the product would inform. Price volatility beating supplier management and credit says the pain is about timing and cost of inputs, which is precisely what the engine computes.

Working capital is the binding constraint, not the motivation

Recordent, Indian SME Receivables Report 2026 · ~1.1 lakh MSMEs · 10 lakh+ transaction-level data points

MeasureFinding
Average invoice payment cycle73 days, against the 45-day statutory limit
Receivables overdue 360+ days₹3.83 crore per firm on average
Invoices issued at 0–30 day terms82.6%
MSME Samadhaan, pending₹22,363.40 crore as of 17 July 2025, from 2.18 lakh applications since 2017

That third row carries the argument. Because 82.6% of invoices already carry terms of 30 days or less, the 73-day reality is buyer collection failure, not MSMEs offering generous credit.

Design constraint, not just context

The engine will routinely compute "buy now, prices are rising" for a firm with ₹3.83 crore locked in ageing receivables and no cash to act on it. Being told to do the right thing you cannot afford destroys trust quickly. The working capital ceiling has to be a hard bound in the optimiser, not a soft cost term.

Headline credit quality is improving, but not for this segment

TransUnion CIBIL and SIDBI, MSME Pulse · March 2025 and December 2025 editions

Aggregate delinquencies fell to a five-year low of 1.79% in March 2025 on a ₹35.2 trillion portfolio growing 13% year on year, and stood at 1.87% in December 2025. That is the headline, and it is misleading for our purposes.

Read the segmentation instead. The improvement came from the ₹50 lakh to ₹50 crore exposure band. The sub-₹50 lakh segment deteriorated, and unsecured business loans originated in March 2025 showed 2.9x higher early delinquency than the overall book.

Implication

The smallest firms in the target market are the stressed pocket even while the sector average looks healthy. Expect price sensitivity and short payback expectations at the micro end.

Concentration risk, with a documented 2025 failure

ICRA, SIAM and ACMA coverage of the rare-earth magnet export controls, 2025 · NITI Aayog on pharmaceutical API dependency

The rare-earth magnet episode is the best-documented recent disruption, and it landed in exactly the tier-2 and tier-3 supplier layer this product would serve.

SignalDetail
Inventory heldOnly 2 to 4 weeks of NdFeB magnet stock across manufacturers
ICRA projectionAutomotive magnet inventories to run out by mid-July 2025 after China tightened export controls
SIAM warningICE passenger-vehicle production cuts from July 2025; electric two-wheeler stocks of 1 to 1.5 months
Policy responseCabinet approved a ₹7,280 crore rare-earth permanent magnet scheme, November 2025
Pharma inputsNITI Aayog puts China dependence at 65% of APIs and key starting materials; China was 43.45% of India's pharma imports in 2023-24

ACMA called the episode a "wake-up call" for auto component makers. Two to four weeks of cover is the whole argument for lead-time modelling: at that buffer, a three-week shift in a supplier's lead time is a production stop, not an inconvenience.

The adoption barrier is behavioural, not financial

India SME Forum, Breaking Barriers, Building Futures: The State of Digitalisation in Indian MSMEs (META Report Card 2025) · 7,835 respondents across 28 states and union territories

MeasureFinding
Digitised core operations53.8% (4,218 of 7,835)
See digital as mission-criticalOnly 28.0% of the full sample
Primary barrier cited36% say resistance to adopting new technology, not cost
Implication for go-to-market

This should shape onboarding and pricing more than any modelling decision. A cheaper product does not solve a behavioural barrier. A product that demands no change in behaviour might, which argues for reading the existing ERP or Tally data rather than asking anyone to enter anything.

Two numbers not to repeat

Superseded
Logistics is not 13–14% of GDP. The DPIIT and NCAER assessment, India's first systematic study, puts it at 7.97% of GDP in FY24 (₹24.01 lakh crore), down from 8.84% in FY23. The older figure came from partial datasets. India now sits alongside the United States at 8.8% and Germany at 8%. Quoting the stale number in front of an informed investor costs credibility.
Disputed
Do not quote a single API-dependency percentage as settled. NITI Aayog says 65% of APIs and key starting materials. Other credible sources in the same search said 70% and "over 80%". Cite the NITI Aayog figure and acknowledge the range.

The gap in the evidence

No credible India-specific data was found on stockout frequency, inventory days, or supplier lead-time variability for MSME manufacturers. It does not appear to be systematically measured. Every source above measures money (credit, receivables, payment days) rather than materials.

Both a problem and the opportunity

The problem cannot be sized from public data, which makes the fundraising conversation harder. But it also means the operational dataset this product would generate does not currently exist in India. That is a stronger moat argument than any forecasting technique, and it pairs well with the 55.7% price-volatility finding as third-party proof that the pain is real.
